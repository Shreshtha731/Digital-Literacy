The guide is broken down into three main categories:

1. Student Toolbox
     This section focuses on the practical tools needed for academic success.

     Organization & Notes: Mentions productivity heavyweights like Notion, Obsidian, and Trello.

     Academic Research: Highlights Google Scholar and Zotero for finding and managing sources.

Programming Basics: Points students toward Codecademy and GitHub for developing technical skills.

2. Safe Communication
     This column deals with the "soft skills" and etiquette of being online.

     Email Etiquette: Focuses on professional communication.

     Social Media Awareness: Reminds students to be mindful of their permanent "digital footprint."

     Cyberbullying Prevention: Encourages maintaining safety and kindness in online interactions.

3. Digital Security
     The final section is all about technical safety and protecting one's identity.

     Password Security: The basics of protecting personal accounts.

     Public Wi-Fi Safety: Tips for secure browsing while on shared networks.

     Phishing Awareness: Teaching students how to spot and avoid online scams.

Overall, the image defines digital literacy not just as knowing how to use software, but as understanding the responsibilities that come with being a "digital citizen."
